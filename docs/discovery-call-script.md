# Discovery Call Script — Dental Vertical
**369 Agentic Systems | Sales Playbook**
**Call length:** 15-20 minutes. Don't go over unless they're clearly buying.

---

## BEFORE THE CALL (2 minutes of prep)

Pull up in separate tabs:
1. Their practice website
2. Their Google Maps listing (reviews, rating, number of locations)
3. Their Gumloop audit results if you ran one
4. The 369 dashboard (logged in, ready to demo)
5. Your pricing doc

Know before you dial:
- Doctor's name and how to pronounce it
- Practice name and city
- How many locations (if visible online)
- Any recent Google reviews mentioning wait times or communication

---

## THE CALL

### OPEN (0:00 — 1:00)
*Warm, confident, not salesy. You called them — own it.*

> "Hi [First Name], Chris Damon here from 369 Agentic Systems — thanks for making time. I'll keep this tight, 15 minutes like I promised. Quick context: I reached out because I ran a scan on [practice name].com and wanted to share what I found — but before I do, I want to make sure this is actually relevant to you. Mind if I ask a couple quick questions first?"

**Why this works:** You're leading with their data, not your product. And asking permission to ask questions gets a yes immediately — starts them saying yes.

---

### DISCOVERY (1:00 — 6:00)
*Goal: Understand their actual pain before you pitch anything.*

**Question 1 — Size/context:**
> "How many providers do you have, and roughly how many active patients?"

*Listen for: Solo practice vs. group. 500 patients = different pain than 3,000.*

---

**Question 2 — The pain question:**
> "What does patient communication look like day-to-day for your front desk? Like — what takes the most time or causes the most friction?"

*Listen for:*
- Insurance questions → Variation 2 angle (staff time)
- New patient inquiries → Variation 3 angle (response speed)
- Appointment scheduling chaos → scheduling angle
- "It's fine actually" → probe deeper or disqualify early

*If they say "it's fine":*
> "Totally fair. Just out of curiosity — when a new patient emails you after hours, what happens? Does someone respond same day, or does it wait until morning?"

*This almost always surfaces the pain.*

---

**Question 3 — Quantify it:**
> "If you had to guess — how many hours a week is your front desk spending on patient emails specifically?"

*Listen for: 5+ hours/week = clear ROI story. Under 3 = smaller practice, adjust pitch.*

---

**Question 4 — The money question (optional, use if they're engaged):**
> "And do you have a sense of how many new patient inquiries come in per month — calls, emails, web form submissions?"

*Listen for: Any number here gives you the ROI math for the close.*

---

### TRANSITION (6:00 — 7:00)
*Pivot from their pain to your solution. Don't pitch yet — just connect.*

> "Okay, that's helpful. What you're describing is exactly the pattern I see in most practices your size — and it's exactly the problem we built for. Can I show you what this looks like in practice? It'll take about 5 minutes."

---

### DEMO (7:00 — 13:00)
*Screen share. Pull up the 369 dashboard. Walk through a live scenario.*

**Step 1 — Set the scene:**
> "So imagine it's 8pm and a patient just emailed asking whether their upcoming crown is covered by their insurance. Your front desk is gone. Here's what happens in our system..."

**Step 2 — Show the pending responses section:**
> "The email comes in here. Our AI reads it, pulls their patient record from your Dentrix system — so it actually knows their insurance provider, their upcoming appointment, their treatment history — and drafts a response. Watch..."

*Click into a pending response card. Expand it.*

> "Here's the original email. And here's what the AI drafted. It mentioned their specific insurance, referenced their upcoming appointment next Thursday, and ended with a clear next step."

**Step 3 — Show the AUTHORIZE button:**
> "You or your office manager gets a notification. You read the draft — takes 10 seconds. If it looks good, you hit Authorize. It sends from your practice's email address. If you want to change something, you edit it right here first."

**Step 4 — Show the audit trail:**
> "Every response is logged. You can see what was sent, when, and to who. Nothing goes out without a human saying yes."

---

**If they ask "what if the AI gets it wrong?":**
> "That's exactly why we built the approval step. Nothing sends automatically. You're always the last check before it reaches a patient. The AI saves the time — you maintain the quality."

**If they ask "does it connect to our Dentrix?":**
> "Yes — that's what makes the responses actually useful instead of generic. We connect to your Dentrix during setup, so when a patient emails, the AI already knows who they are, when their next appointment is, and what's in their treatment plan."

---

### CLOSE (13:00 — 18:00)
*Read the room. If they're engaged, go straight to pricing. Don't ask "what do you think?" — it invites stalling.*

**Transition to price:**
> "Here's how we work. There's a one-time setup fee to get everything connected — your email, your Dentrix, your approval workflow — and then a flat monthly retainer. Setup is $3,000, monthly is $1,500."

*Pause. Let them respond. Don't fill the silence.*

---

**If they say "that seems like a lot":**
> "I get it. Let me put it in perspective — you said you get roughly [X] new patient inquiries a month. If faster response time converts even two more of those per month, at [$1,500 average patient value], that's $3,000 in additional revenue. The monthly retainer pays for itself before the end of month one."

---

**If they say "I need to think about it":**
> "Of course. What's the main thing you'd want to think through? I'd rather answer it now than have you go back and forth on something I can clear up in 30 seconds."

*Listen. Most "think about it" objections are one of three things:*
- Price → ROI math above
- Trust ("will this actually work?") → offer a trial / first month refund guarantee
- Decision-maker not on the call → "Who else would need to be part of this decision? Can we get them on a quick call this week?"

---

**If they say "yes, let's do it":**
> "Great. I'll send over the agreement today — it's straightforward, two pages. Once that's signed I'll schedule the setup call. We can usually have everything live within 24 hours of setup."

*Send the MSA same day. Don't wait.*

---

**If they're not a fit (disqualify cleanly):**
> "Honestly, based on what you've described, I'm not sure the timing is right for this. You'd get the most value if [X condition]. When that changes, reach back out — I'd rather earn your business when it makes sense than push something that doesn't fit right now."

*This builds more trust than a hard push on a wrong-fit prospect.*

## OBJECTION QUICK REFERENCE

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "We already have someone handling emails" | "How long does it take them? We're not replacing them — we're giving them 2 hours a day back." |
| "We're not sure about AI handling patient communication" | "Nothing sends without you approving it. You're always the last check." |
| "We use Eaglesoft, not Dentrix" | "We're adding Eaglesoft integration next quarter. I can put you on the early access list." |
| "Can we try it first?" | "Absolutely — if after the first month it's not saving time, I'll refund the setup fee. No contract lock-in." |
| "We're too small" | "How many patient emails do you handle per week?" — if 20+, it's worth it. If less, disqualify gracefully. |
| "Send me more information" | "Happy to. What specifically would be most useful — pricing details, how the Dentrix connection works, or what setup looks like?" |
